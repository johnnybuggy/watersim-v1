Display

ⓘ About auto detail

Auto tests increasing grid, particle and rendering detail with a 20% frame-budget reserve. The highest passing tier stays fixed; render scale adapts without resetting water. Targets are not guarantees. Recalibrate after changing physics or window size.

Planet size

ⓘ About planet size

Radius sizes the rocky globe (0.5–25 m — sea level, terrain relief, gravity, balls, camera and sky all scale with it, so every size behaves like the default world); ocean volume scales how much water fills it (particle count ×0.1–×50 of the active detail preset); bumpiness 0 gives a smooth voxel ball. Applies on release.

Orbit & rotation

ⓘ About orbit, day/night & seasons

The Sun is fixed; the planet revolves around it (one “year”) and spins about its vertical axis (one “day” — the day/night cycle). The spin axis can be tilted against the orbit (axial tilt — the tilted axis keeps its direction in space, so the year brings seasons: each hemisphere leans toward the Sun on opposite sides of the orbit). Shorter period = faster motion.

Ocean dynamics

ⓘ About the atmosphere & water cycle

Eddies keep whirling after a splash; slow streams wander beneath the surface. Sun activity heats the lit side and drives evaporation: every water surface parcel evaporates with probability growing exponentially from the coldest water temperature to the hottest, so the night side evaporates too — just much slower. Vapor cloudlets ride circulating winds, stay aloft on a buoyant layer, and bounce off the atmosphere ceiling (probability rising with height) and off the terrain. In shade vapor cools, condenses and rains back down; dense vapor pockets crackle with lightning — the thunderstorm slider scales how easily they fire (×0.01–×10, logarithmic, ×0.1 by default). The atmosphere height is the REAL ceiling: evaporated particles and droplets can rise exactly that far above sea level, on any planet size.
